Every row cites its instrument, gives a one-sentence rationale and a confidence, by the same rule

silent or the claim is disputed.

### The NRCan check does not reach this era

NRCan's *Territorial Evolution* service publishes **vector layers only from 1867**; earlier years
exist as scanned rasters only. So every pre-1867 row says so in the checklist, and the comparison
is replaced by **34 control points** — a place, a date, and the unit the instruments put it in,
each with its citation (`checks:` in `events.yaml`, tolerance 0.01°). All 34 pass. They cover
Québec and York Factory in 1690, Louisbourg and Annapolis Royal in 1720, the Proclamation line in
1765, the Quebec Act's reach in 1780, Fredericton and Sydney in 1790, the Ottawa River line in
1795, the Labrador coast in 1815 and 1830, the Magdalen Islands' exception in 1815, Kamloops and
the Rockies in 1860, and the Stickeen and 1863 lines.

One control point was wrong and the atlas was right: the Magdalen Islands point `[-61.75, 47.40]`
is open water in the Atlas of Canada 1:1M coverage. Havre-aux-Maisons `[-61.79, 47.42]` is land.

### Sovereigns and the de facto layer

- Rows now carry a **sovereign**: Britain, France, England, Spain, the Hudson's Bay Company,
  Indigenous nations, Canada, and the shared forms ("Britain and France", "England and France").
- The **de facto layer** for this era is fur-post catchments and settlement belts, from
  `pipeline/atlas/defacto.yaml`: **94 posts**, each with its open periods, the power holding it and
  a tier that sets its catchment radius (bay factory 60 km, depot 80 km, post 50 km, outpost
  25 km, French Great Lakes 50 km, French west 40 km, Pacific 30 km), and **22 settlement belts**,
  each a line or a set of points with its own width — the St. Lawrence seigneurial rangs, the
  Richelieu, Port-Royal and the Acadian marshlands, Plaisance, Louisbourg, the English Shore,
  Halifax, the Planter and Loyalist valleys, the Upper Canada front, and the Red River lots.
- The layer is off by default and drawn at reduced opacity; every de facto row carries an explicit
  `confidence` (annotations deliberately do not carry across an `alter`, so each one restates it).

### New geometry operators

`buffer` (points or a line, in km), `near_coast` (mainland within so many km of the sea coast,
inside a given area — the 1763 Labrador coastal strip), and `posts` and `belts` (catchments and
corridors of a power's posts and settlements open in a given year, from `defacto.yaml`). All are
unit-tested.

### Open questions logged

- **Interior Labrador, 1763–1867, has three incompatible readings:** NRCan's 1974 plate puts it in
  Quebec/Lower Canada, the JCPC's 1927 judgment reads the 1763 Proclamation as giving the whole
  watershed to Newfoundland, and Sitting A leaves it in Rupert's Land. The atlas keeps the
  Rupert's Land reading for now; Sitting C draws the overlap hatched.
- **The "two Districts of Keewatin" phrase is Surveyor General Deville's (1899)**, not the
  Minister of Justice's. The 1897 row's note says so.
- **British Columbia's capital on 1 July 1867 was New Westminster**, not Victoria; a 1868-05-25
  event moves it.

## C. Sitting C — pre-contact and disputed

Branch `phase-2c`.

### The contact frontier

- **`pipeline/atlas/contact.yaml`: 65 sourced entries**, each a year, an area expression and a
  citation. A place takes the **earliest** year covering it, so entries compose in any order.
- **The 25 StatCan drainage basins are the backbone**, each carrying its interior's *latest* date,
  with coasts, rivers, islands and posts cutting through. Measured coverage: 9,652,844 km² against
  Canada's 9,650,830 km² — gapless, which is a property of the partition rather than of care.
- **Bands:** before 1500 (108,975 km²), 1500–1599 (649,199), 1600–1699 (1,233,899), 1700–1799
  (2,474,174), 1800–1899 (4,430,511), 1900 and after (756,086).
- **Its own artefact pair,** `contact.v1.json` (21 KB) and `contact.v1.topojson.gz` (174 KB),
  fetched only when the layer is asked for. A band's edge is not a boundary and must not be snapped
  to one.
- **`first_contact_year` is now a per-hex attrs column** (measure, unit `year`).
- **The caveat is required by the schema** and shown with the layer.
- **Four journey corridors were left out on purpose** — Hearne, Henday, de Troyes, Albanel. The
  years are solid, the routes are not, and drawing them from prose would be tracing a guess.

### The pre-contact base

- Native Land stays gated. The permission flag is read from `pipeline/artefacts.yaml` at build
  time, so the pipeline and the app cannot disagree. Until it says `granted` the base is plain and
  says why; the code path that loads their layer exists and is exercised by a unit test.
- The timeline starts at 1000, 1497 or the contact frontier, defaulting to 1497.

### The disputed layer

Six disputes, hatched, each row naming its claimant; the panel lists every claimant of a dispute,
so a hatch is never anonymous.

| Dispute | Rows | Opened | Closed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oregon Country | United States' claim; Britain's Columbia District | 1819-01-30 | 1846-07-17 |
| San Juan Islands | one row, both claims (the ground is the same islands) | 1846-07-17 | 1872-10-21 |
| Labrador | Canada (for Quebec); Newfoundland | 1809-03-30 | 1927-03-01 |
| Ontario–Manitoba | Manitoba's eastern claim; Ontario's northern claim | 1881-07-01 | 1889-08-12 |
| Sverdrup Islands | Norway (Sverdrup claim) | 1902-09-19 | 1930-11-05 |
| Hans Island | Canada; Kingdom of Denmark | 1973-12-17 | 2022-06-14 |
| Machias Seal and the grey zone | one row, both claims | 1977-01-01 | **open** |

- **`clip:`** was added to the build (`canada`, `north_america`, `none`) because most of the ground
  in these disputes is now American; clipping a claim to Canada would draw it as already settled.
- **Hans Island is also a de jure change:** the 2022 agreement takes the eastern part into
  Greenland, so Nunavut is cut. It is the atlas's last event and Canada's first new land boundary
  since 1949.
- **The Alaska panhandle is deliberately not drawn.** The 1825 convention fixes no coordinate
  between 56°N and 141°W, and the 1903 tribunal answered by marking a map, so the Canadian claim
  exists only on Map 37 of the British atlas. A ±5–10 km tracing would be the one thing this atlas
  refuses to do; the 1903 event says so in its note.
- **NRCan has a counterpart for one of these only** — its "Disputed area" polygon for
  Ontario–Manitoba, which the existing de jure row already reproduces. The checklist now says
  "no NRCan counterpart (their maps draw boundaries, not claims)" against the rest.
