# 2026-10-09 — Release 1.0.5: city hexes, water that reads as water, and settlement dates (branch `hexes-1.0.5`)

Decisions are under **2026-10-09 — Release 1.0.5** in `docs/decisions.md`. `pre-1.0.5` was tagged on
`87b59ee` before any change. Released files were not touched. Everything below is in new files, and
`hexes.r4.v1` and its layer rebuild byte-identical after `hexes.py` was refactored to share its
aggregation.

## Before building
- Read `docs/interop.md` (unit tables, rule 7), the 1.0.3 and 1.0.4 decisions and logs,
  `pipeline/unittables.py` and `pipeline/hexes.py`.
- **Three new sources**, all pinned in the manifest:
  - `nrcan_atlas_waterbodies_1m`, the Atlas of Canada 1:1M lakes (OGL – Canada), sha256 `f39a35561718…`;
  - `nrcan_atlas_islands_1m`, its islands, sha256 `169460186ad4…`;
  - `wikidata_csd_dates`, the query `pipeline/csd_dates.rq` (CC0), 12,087 rows. Its sorted CSV is
    1,246,826 bytes with sha256 `40f87d072c05…`, fetched 2026-10-09.
- **Memory.** The editor's processes (Pylance, the TypeScript servers) hold about 2.5 GB. A first
  version of the neighbour rule closed the land of seven hexagons at once, and the host terminated it
  in the Arctic (SIGTERM). The rule now closes each cell once at its own size. `hexboard.py` takes
  about 12 minutes and peaks at 1,761 MB RSS; the lowest available memory during it was 1,463 MB.

## Built, by brief item
1. **Water that reads as water.** `pipeline/hexboard.py` writes `data/build/hexes.r4.v1.2.json.gz`
   (482,479 bytes, 5,986 rows) and `data/build/layers/hexes.r4.v1.2.topojson.gz` (737,160 bytes).
   `pipeline/landlinks.py` does the land and the rule.
   - **The lakes.** All 57 large lakes are water: named, permanent, 1,000 km² or more, not a river.
     The 13 the CSDs already leave out keep the CSDs' shore. The other 44 are taken out of the land,
     less the Atlas islands lying mostly inside them: 161,241 km². All ten named lakes are water.
     Lake of the Woods is one of the 13: the CSDs hold 20% of it, and those parts are islands.
   - **Rows.** 25 parents have no land and are not rows: v1's 21 Great Lakes rows, and 4 in Great
     Slave Lake and Lake Winnipeg. Nobody lives in them.
   - **The neighbour rule** is stated exactly in `docs/interop.md`, "The neighbour rule (1.0.5)":
     land closed by 600 m, a landmass read in the hexagon and its six neighbours, and a link that is
     land where the principal lands meet on the edge. The gap is **600 m, not 2 km** (below).
   - **Everything else** is as in v1 over the new land, plus `landPoint`.
2. **City hexes.** `data/build/hexes.r5.v1.json.gz` (50,328 bytes, 407 rows) and
   `data/build/layers/hexes.r5.v1.topojson.gz` (32,960 bytes), unit `h3_r5`.
   - Every field in the brief, plus `landPoint`.
   - The 62 parents of 100,000 or more have 415 mesh cells. 407 have land; the 8 without are in Lake
     Ontario and hold nobody. A parent's population and places are exactly its cells'.
3. **Dates.** `pipeline/settled.py` reads the pinned Wikidata result. It fills `settledYear`,
   `settledPlace` and `settledSource` on every resolution-4 row, and `cityYear`, `cityPlace` and
   `citySource` on the 62 rows of 100,000 or more. The rules are in `docs/interop.md`, "Settlement
   dates". The coverage report is at the end of this log.
4. **Contract and docs.**
   - The Zod schema adds `h3_r5` to the union and optional fields to `h3_r4`; it is exported to
     `docs/schemas/unitTable.schema.json`.
   - `checkRows` and `validate.py` allow city-hex neighbours outside the table and check each year
     against its source.
   - `artefacts.yaml`, `SHA256SUMS` (4 new files, the 22 others unchanged), `make hexboard` in `make
     build` and `make verify`, three rows in `docs/data-sources.md`, and the licences page.
   - `docs/interop.md` ("The 1.0.5 hex board"), the CHANGELOG, the decisions, this log and the
     pipeline README.
   - Every changed link is listed in `docs/log/2026-10-09-links.md`.

## The gap: 600 m, not 2 km
The brief's named cases cannot all hold at about 2 km. The widest water on the best way between each
pair, on the land above:

| Pair | Widest water on the way | Brief | At 600 m |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vancouver Island – mainland (Seymour Narrows, Discovery Islands) | 709 m | water | water |
| Manitoulin – mainland (Little Current) | 471 m | land | land |
| Québec City – Lévis | 622 m | — | water |
| Montréal – Laval, Montréal – south shore, Laval – north shore | 101, 166, 61 m | land | land |
| Cape Breton – mainland (causeway) | 0 m | land | land |
| North shore – south shore of the St Lawrence east of Île d'Orléans | 4,732 m | water | water |
| Prince Edward Island – New Brunswick | 12,695 m | water | water |
| Newfoundland – Labrador | 17,125 m | water | water |

At 2 km, the Discovery Islands join Vancouver Island to the mainland. The named cases leave only a
gap between 471 and 709 m, so the build uses 600 m. A decision for Mark is in `docs/decisions.md`.
