## 1670-05-12 — The Hudson's Bay Company charter

Charles II grants the Hudson's Bay Company the lands draining into Hudson Bay, as Rupert's Land. France holds Canada and Acadia, and England and France both fish and claim Newfoundland. The charter is dated 2 May 1670 in the Old Style calendar England then used.

## 1713-04-11 — The Treaty of Utrecht

France gives up Hudson Bay, Newfoundland and "Nova Scotia or Acadia, with its ancient boundaries" to Great Britain, and keeps Cape Breton and the islands of the Gulf as Île Royale. The two powers never agree what Acadia's ancient boundaries were. Signed 31 March 1713 in the Old Style calendar; the exchange of ratifications was within four weeks but its day is not established.

## 1763-10-07 — The Royal Proclamation

Britain creates the Province of Quebec as a narrow band along the St. Lawrence and reserves the lands beyond the colonies for Indigenous nations. Newfoundland is given the Labrador coast, and Nova Scotia the islands of the Gulf.

## 1784-05-12 — The Treaty of Paris with the United States

Britain recognizes the United States, with a boundary through the Great Lakes and along the highlands south of the St. Lawrence. The treaty's vague lines leave disputes over New Brunswick and the country west of Lake Superior; nothing inside modern Canada changes hands. Signed 3 September 1783; in force 12 May 1784.

No polygon changes on this date.
